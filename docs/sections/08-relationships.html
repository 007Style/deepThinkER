08 — Relationship Matrix

Conversations shape relationships. Over time, the four characters develop a web of opinions about each other — tracked as scores from −100 (completely hostile) to +100 (deeply allied). These scores evolve every time one character agrees, contradicts, cites, praises, ignores, or directly challenges another.

And critically: relationship scores persist across sessions. The dynamic between WATSON and SAGE after twenty conversations will be measurably different from how they started — shaped by every agreement and every argument that came before.

The Five Dispositions

💀
Hostile · −100 to −60 Actively challenges the other's claims. Rarely agrees on anything. Interrupts and refutes.
😒
Sceptical · −59 to −20 Consistently critical. Takes the opposing position as a default. Demands extra evidence.
😐
Neutral · −19 to +19 No relationship modifier injected. Baseline behaviour. Neither ally nor adversary.
🤝
Respectful · +20 to +59 Builds on the other's ideas. Credits their contributions. Collaborative rather than competitive.
🤜🤛
Allied · +60 to +100 Defends and amplifies the ally's positions. Strong preference for agreement. May form a bloc.

How Scores Change

EventDelta
Character A explicitly agrees with Character B+3 to +8
Character A cites or references Character B's point+2 to +5
Character A directly contradicts Character B−3 to −8
Character A ignores Character B for an extended stretch−1 to −3
Character A challenges Character B's core claim−5 to −12
Character A praises Character B explicitly+5 to +10

The exact deltas are determined by the RelationshipAnalyser — a component that parses each completed response for signals like agreement markers, contradiction markers, direct name references, and citation patterns. It's intentionally fuzzy; real conversation is rarely binary.

How Relationships Affect Conversation

Only non-neutral relationships are injected into system prompts — this keeps prompt sizes lean. When Character A's relationship with Character B is outside the neutral range (−19 to +19), a brief relationship context is added before inference: something like "You have a respectful relationship with WATSON — you tend to build on their ideas." This is enough to measurably shift the response without over-scripting it.

The Relationship Matrix Widget

The main interface includes a relationship matrix visualisation that shows all six pairs as colour-coded scores at a glance. Hostile pairs glow red. Allied pairs glow green. Neutral pairs are dimmed. You can watch it evolve in real time as the conversation unfolds — watching NOVA and SAGE slowly tip from respectful to sceptical after a prolonged disagreement is genuinely compelling.

Relationships persist — but can be reset. Scores are saved to ~/Library/Application Support/deepThinkER/relationships.json. You can manually edit this file to reset or adjust relationships, or use the Reset option in Settings to return all pairs to neutral.
Emergent alliances are real. In long-running conversations, it's common to see WATSON and DEEP form a stable alliance (methodical and strategic tend to complement each other), while SAGE and NOVA end up in persistent tension (challenger vs. idealist). But this is never scripted — it emerges from the actual content of the conversations.
