12 — Architecture Deep-Dive

deepThinkER is built on a deliberate set of architectural principles designed to keep the codebase maintainable, testable, and free from third-party framework lock-in. Understanding these principles makes the codebase approachable for contributors and illuminates why certain decisions were made.

Core Design Principles

Pure-Dart Core Everything in lib/core/ is zero-Flutter. No import 'package:flutter/…' anywhere in core. This makes all business logic purely testable via dart test — no widget infrastructure needed. Core has no knowledge of screens, widgets, or rendering.
No State Management Packages No Riverpod, BLoC, Provider, GetX, or Redux. State flows through vanilla Flutter setState and dart:async streams. The conversation engine exposes a Stream<ConversationEvent> that the UI subscribes to.
Bundled Runtime The Ollama binary and metallib GPU shader files are bundled inside the app bundle via Git LFS. No separate Ollama install. No version mismatch. The app launches Ollama as a subprocess and owns its lifecycle completely.
Minimal Dependencies Only 6 runtime packages: http, path_provider, url_launcher, flutter_local_notifications, audioplayers, archive. No AI framework, no ORM, no reactive package.

The Conversation Engine

The ConversationEngine is the heart of the app. It maintains a pool of four InferenceWorker instances — one per character — and orchestrates turn-taking via jitter delays and pass mechanics. Each worker streams tokens from the Ollama API and emits them as ConversationEvents on a shared broadcast stream. The UI subscribes to this stream and renders tokens as they arrive.

The jitter mechanic means characters don't always respond immediately after the previous one finishes — there's a randomised delay (configurable) that prevents a perfectly round-robin cadence and makes the conversation feel more natural and unpredictable.

Why no sandbox? The app requires com.apple.security.app-sandbox = false in its entitlements because it needs to spawn the Ollama subprocess. macOS App Sandbox prohibits subprocess spawning. This is a known trade-off — the app mitigates the risk through the InjectionGuard, ContentFilter, and domain whitelist rather than OS-level sandboxing.
