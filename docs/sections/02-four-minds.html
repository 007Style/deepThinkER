02 — The Four Minds

Each character in deepThinkER is more than a system prompt with a name attached. They have a distinct reasoning style, a default emotional register, a private memory store, an independent trust score, and a specific approach to using the internet. They don't just sound different — they are different, all the way down to the model they run on.

All four are named in honour of landmark IBM AI achievements — a nod to the long, strange history of machines that think.

How the Characters Actually Differ

The differences between the four aren't cosmetic. They run on different models (Gemma 2, Phi-3, Llama 3, Mistral), each with genuinely different strengths, context lengths, and response styles. Their system prompts are carefully crafted to draw out the personality each model is most capable of expressing. And their trust scores, mood states, and relationship scores evolve independently — so two sessions on the same topic will feel different because the characters have changed between them.

Want to customise them? Every character's model and master prompt can be changed in the Startup Config screen before each session. Hit "Reset to Defaults" at any time to restore the carefully tuned IBM-character prompts. You can even create a completely new persona — give them a different name, a different model, and a whole new personality.

The Conversation Dynamic

In a typical session, DEEP tends to open threads and frame the big picture. WATSON digs into specifics, checks facts, and grounds claims in evidence. NOVA pulls the conversation sideways into unexpected territory. SAGE waits for a claim to get overconfident, then dismantles it with surgical precision. It's not scripted — the order and intensity of contributions is driven by jitter delays, mood states, relationship scores, and the live streaming outputs of the models themselves.

You can jump in at any time. Your messages are injected into the shared context and every character sees them — and responds in their own way. WATSON might immediately start fact-checking your claim. NOVA might ask a disorienting follow-up. SAGE might politely but firmly tell you that you're wrong.
